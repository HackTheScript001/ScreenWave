What's new in 1.3.0
- Remove apps on 2021 and newer TVs: these only allow removal through the TV's own Apps settings, so ScreenWave now does it with the TV's remote – it opens Apps › Settings, moves to each app, deletes it, and checks the TV's app list to confirm the right app went. It stops straight away if anything unexpected happens.
- Connect remote is now on the Remove apps page too.
- For SecondWave: Teach removal (Teach mode) records how one app is removed on a TV, and confirms the app grid layout before it's ever used automatically.

What's new in 1.2.0
- Do it for me now uses button presses built into ScreenWave for your TV's year, so there's nothing to set up – click it and the remote does it. (Recordings for 2021 TVs are coming in the next update.)
- Teach is now behind a Teach mode switch, for recording new TV years.

What's new in 1.1.1
- Remove apps: uses the app ID the TV's remove command expects (2021 TVs answered "can not uninstall app" before), and tries the other ID forms if one is refused.
- The firewall lock status now shows at the bottom of the window as well as on About.

What's new in 1.1.0
- Remove apps now reads the app list from 2021 and newer TVs, and shows how often each app has been opened. Apps that have never been opened are suggested for removal.
- Do it for me: connect ScreenWave as a remote (the TV asks you to Allow it once), teach a setting once with the on-screen remote, and ScreenWave repeats it for you on any TV in the same model range. Recordings stay on your PC and can be exported to a file.
- Settings: add your AdGuard Home or Pi-hole address, and Stop apps coming back can add the block list for you and open the page so you can see it. Your existing rules are kept. Passwords are only saved if you choose, encrypted for your Windows account.
- Firewall lock: the installer adds Windows Firewall rules so ScreenWave and Samsung's sdb can only reach your local network – never the internet. About shows that it's on.
- App names with symbols (like Tubi's) now show correctly.

What's new in 1.0.1
- Clearer steps for turning on the TV's Developer Mode: what to press, what you'll see on the TV, and this PC's number shown big to type in.

What's new in 1.0.0
- First version of ScreenWave: speeds up Samsung Tizen TVs by removing the preloaded apps you don't use, through the TV's Developer Mode.
- Connect over your home network, or with a cable straight from your PC to the TV – the TV never needs the internet.
- Lists the TV's apps, separates the ones that can be removed from Samsung's system apps (which are never touched), suggests the usual unused ones, and confirms each removal.
- Step-by-step speed-up settings checklist, a block list to stop removed apps coming back, and a finish-up guide to turn Developer Mode off again.
- Fully offline: ScreenWave never connects to the internet and sends nothing about you or your TV anywhere.
